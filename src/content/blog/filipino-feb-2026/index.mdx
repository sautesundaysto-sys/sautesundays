Pulutan is the Filipino word for the food you eat while drinking, and *Pulutan! Filipino Bar Bites, Appetizers and Street Food* by Marvin Gapultos is a whole book of it. Nothing in there is a formal course. It is all designed to show up in waves and be picked at while everyone talks over each other, which is a fair description of what we were going for in February.

![The table at Sauté Sundays Filipino night](/events/filipino-feb-2026/event1.jpeg)

Michael made the grilled pork belly skewers, glazed with coffee and ginger beer, which sounds like a stunt and absolutely was not. Clarice made the chicken fried eggplant in a tray big enough to share properly. Leena brought corn dogged quail eggs, Fran a red tub of garlic vinegar peanuts under a heap of cilantro, Silkan the pineapple pigs in a blanket, and Deepak the fried calamari with a lemon wedge and a bowl of sauce. Almost everything on that counter was fried, and somehow it did not feel heavy, because you were only ever taking two of a thing.

It made for one of our loudest tables. Plates kept appearing, nothing arrived on a schedule, and the evening stretched well past when we thought it would end.

February is a hard month in Toronto, and a warm, noisy room full of food was a good answer to it. Thank you to everyone who cooked and to everyone who stayed late.
