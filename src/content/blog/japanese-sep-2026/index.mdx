September was *Mastering the Art of Japanese Home Cooking*, and it filled the room more than most months do.

![The table at Sauté Sundays September night, food running the length of it while guests serve themselves](./event1.jpg)

The food ran the whole length of the table and people just kept moving down it. Slow cookers and rice cookers still plugged in, carried across the city that afternoon by somebody who had decided their dish needed to arrive hot. Foil trays two deep in places. Little wooden card holders next to each one so you knew what you were reaching for before you committed.

![Guests filling their plates along the table, foil trays and bowls spread the length of it](./event2.jpg)

Japanese home cooking turned out to suit the way we actually eat. Almost nothing needed plating. It was all meant to be picked at, so the line down the table never properly stopped, and most people went back two or three times rather than building one enormous plate at the start.

![The line of guests working their way down the table, a rice cooker and a slow cooker among the dishes](./event3.jpg)

We ran out of room on the table before we ran out of dishes, which is the correct problem to have.
