*My Big Fat Greek Cookbook* was the pick for March, and it is a good example of a book that reads easier than it eats. Greek cooking is deceptively simple on paper. Olive oil, lemon, oregano, a short ingredient list. Then you taste six versions of it side by side and realise how much room there is inside those constraints.

![The Greek table at Sauté Sundays](/events/greek-mar-2026/event1.jpg)

Marie made the kebab, Meghna a pork stew, and Louise a spinach and rice that did the quiet work every table needs. This was one of our fuller tables. A lot of dishes, all of them wanting to be eaten at the same time. The mezze approach helped, because nothing needed plating properly. Everything went down the middle and stayed there. There was a lot of bread, and none of it survived.

![Dishes from Greek night](/events/greek-mar-2026/event2.jpg)

Dessert was the best of the year so far. Karen and Clarice both made a cake, one orange and one apple, entirely separately, and between them they were my favourite thing anyone brought that night. I made the orange one again for my birthday party.

![More from the March table](/events/greek-mar-2026/event3.jpg)

I think this is also one of the favourites from our community. People actually bought the book after the event, because the recipes are so easy for how nice the dishes turn out. That is the highest compliment a month can get. Nobody buys a cookbook because a night was fun. They buy it because they want to make the thing again.

![The table at Greek night](/events/greek-mar-2026/event4.jpg)

March is the month Toronto starts to turn, and this felt like the first table of the year looking forward rather than hunkering down. Thank you to everyone who cooked.
