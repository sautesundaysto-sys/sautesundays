*Win Son Presents a Taiwanese American Cookbook* comes from the Brooklyn restaurant of the same name, and it is less a survey of Taiwanese cooking than a record of what two cooks actually make. That suited a table like ours, which runs on lots of small things all meant to be shared.

![Sauté Sundays September recap](/events/taiwan-sep-2025/event1.png)

The spread leaned right into it. Beef rolls came rolled and sliced, gua bao arrived already filled and stacked on a plate, and a bowl of clams sat next to a tray of shrimp skewers that went almost immediately. Someone brought wood ear mushrooms under a handful of cilantro, someone else a tray of scallion bread cut into squares, and there were cups of chopped greens dressed and portioned so nobody had to serve themselves from one big bowl. A pot of soup sat at the end quietly reheating. Blueberry muffins closed it out, which is not Taiwanese in any sense but nobody objected.

This was the month it became obvious the format worked. Nine dishes from one book, no duplicates, and enough variety that you could eat your way from one end of the table to the other and still not try everything.
