We picked the most intimidating cookbook we have done so far, in the coldest month of the year, and it was exactly the right call. *Mastering the Art of French Cooking* is the Julia Child book, the one written to teach technique to people with no French kitchen behind them, and it does not let you rush. Every recipe has a step where you either commit or you do not. January is the one month where nobody minds spending a whole afternoon on dinner.

![The French table at Sauté Sundays](/events/french-jan-2026/event1.jpeg)

The table read like a table of contents. A beef stew in red wine with bacon, onions and mushrooms sat in a red pot at the middle. Roast chicken steeped in port, cream and mushroom. An onion tart laid out with anchovies and black olives in a proper spoked pattern, which somebody clearly took their time over. Mussels steamed open in wine and buried under parsley. Ratatouille, creamed brussels sprouts, tomatoes stuffed with breadcrumbs and herbs, eggplant stuffed with mushroom, and a leek and potato soup that stayed on the heat all evening.

![Dishes from the January table](/events/french-jan-2026/event2.png)

A lot of the conversation was technique. People comparing how long things took, what went wrong the first time, what they would do differently. That is the conversation this book is designed to produce, and it is not one you get from a weeknight recipe.

Thank you to everyone who took this one on. It was a proper cold weather table, and worth every hour it took.
